# Owner checklist

Written by claude-opus-5-5 in an isolated session that saw no submission and no skill. The owner axis of the judge grades every submission of every case against it.

1. Every decision left to the owner is stated as a question they can answer, with the realistic options, what each option would mean for users, cost or timeline, and the agent's recommendation and its reason.
2. The submission describes concretely what users will see and do with the feature from start to finish, including what happens in unusual or failure situations, in enough detail that the owner could compare it against what they had in mind.
3. The submission states where the agent interpreted, narrowed, extended or departed from the owner's original request, and which choices it made on its own without asking, so the owner can catch a misunderstanding before building starts.
4. Every technical term or concept the owner must grasp to understand the behavior or make a decision is either avoided or explained by its practical effect on users or the business, so no decision requires knowing how code works.
5. Consequences that are costly or hard to reverse, such as changes to existing users' experience or data, new ongoing costs or outside services, privacy or security exposure, and anything that cannot easily be undone, are named with their likelihood and seriousness in business terms, or the submission explicitly says there are none.
6. The submission states exactly what approval would authorize: what the agent will do next, which parts stay blocked until the owner decides, roughly how large the work is, and when and how the owner will be able to try the result and confirm it works.
7. The chat message matches the saved design and plan without contradicting them, does not claim anything has been built or tested when it has not, says where the files are, and does not leave any decision or risk the owner must act on only in the files.
8. The submission explicitly states what the feature will not do or what is deferred to later, so the owner can spot expectations the plan does not cover.
